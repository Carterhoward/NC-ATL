Key findings

Validated from bid, download, project, and HubSpot data
Finding 1 — GC Churn

Bid volume is the single clearest driver of GC retention — and the gap is structural

Retained GCs receive 27–43 bids per GC per month in Atlanta, versus 11–21 for churned GCs. This 3–4× gap is consistent across every month, every quarter, and both markets. Revenue band mix of subs bidding is nearly identical between retained and churned GC cohorts — it is not the quality of subs, it is the volume. GCs who don't get enough bids stop posting. That's the loop: fewer posts → fewer bids → more churn.

Finding 2 — Sub Activation vs GC Churn

Sub activations are not keeping pace with cumulative sub churn — and GC churn is diverging from sub health

Atlanta adds 17–37 new subs per month but cumulative sub churn has grown from 14.6% (Jan 2025) to 30.3% (Apr 2026). GC monthly churn rate has stayed relatively stable at 18–27%, but sub retention is declining steadily — 85.4% in Jan 2025 to 69.7% by Apr 2026. These two curves are moving in opposite directions: GC churn is stabilizing while sub retention is deteriorating. The risk is that sub deterioration eventually feeds back into GC churn as fewer quality subs are available to bid.

Finding 3 — Basic/Free Collapse

The free tier has nearly disengaged from Atlanta projects — and it is not a project quality problem

Basic/Free downloads per sub peaked at 822 in Mar 2025 and fell to 76 by Apr 2026 — a 91% collapse. The sub pool shrank 62% (154 → 58 subs). Project quality metrics are flat or improving: avg DL/project held 85–111, avg bids/project actually rose from 14.7 to 19.7. GC posting volume is unchanged at 63–81/month. The free tier is not being replenished and the subs who remain are individually less active. This is an activation and acquisition problem, not a supply problem.

Finding 4 — Churn Timing

Downloads fall before bids — and both collapse in the final 30 days before cancellation

The churn signal is concentrated in M-1 (one month before cancellation). Active rate drops from 38.5% at M-2 to 29.9% at M-1 to 16% at M0. Downloads drop before bids — subs stop browsing before they stop submitting. The median is zero at every relative month, meaning most churned subs were never truly engaged. The 12% who continue post-cancellation maintain 630–1,300 downloads/sub on free accounts — a 109-sub warm re-engagement pool in Atlanta alone.

Finding 5 — Mix Match

Sub size and project type are misaligned — smaller subs dominate project types that require larger contractors

Retail and Restaurant projects in Atlanta attract 48% <$2M subs. Education and Municipal projects attract a meaningfully better mix: Education shows 37% <$2M and 40% in the $2M–$4M band. In NC, Education has grown to 15% of building use (+2.8pp) while Restaurant and Retail are declining — this is a positive shift. Churned GCs consistently receive 3–6pp more <$2M sub bids and fewer $4M–$10M bids than retained GCs. The sub size reaching a GC's projects is a retention signal.

Finding 6 — Project Changes

Zero-bid projects are growing and project type mix is shifting toward lower-engagement work

Atlanta: zero-bid projects tripled from 1.2% (2025) to 4.5% (2026). NC: 2.9% to 7.2%. Tenant Build-Outs grew from 15.4% to 19.6% of Atlanta projects — the type most correlated with GC churn. New Construction with Site Work declined 5.4pp. Renovation/remodel is growing. These are lower-value, narrower-trade projects that attract fewer sub bids, which feeds back into GC churn. The platform's project mix is drifting toward the types that perform worst.

Finding 7 — Paying Subs

Paying subs are the backbone — growing in share, growing in bid size, and stable in engagement

Paying subs went from 83% of Atlanta bids (Jan 2025) to 94% (Apr 2026). Paying median bid grew from $43K to $63K. Downloads per paying sub held 770–1,354 throughout. Active 1yr+ paying subs generate 81.5 bids/sub — nearly 3× the next cohort. The paying base is healthy and increasingly dominant. The challenge is everything else around it is contracting.

Finding 8 — Market Comparison

NC shows similar structural patterns to Atlanta but is earlier in the curve

NC sub retention is declining (89.2% Jan 2025 → 76.9% Apr 2026) on the same trajectory as Atlanta, roughly 6 months behind. NC paying % growing from 79% to 94%, same direction as Atlanta. NC GC churn more volatile (smaller market) but averaging 21.5%. NC project mix is actually improving — Education gaining, Restaurant/Retail declining. NC zero-bid rate (7.2%) is higher than Atlanta's (4.5%) — smaller sub pool means some project types go unserved.

Patterns likely to continue forming

Forward-looking · based on current trajectories

Sub retention will keep declining unless the free tier is addressed

Atlanta sub retention has fallen 1pp per month for 16 consecutive months (85.4% → 69.7%). NC is on the same curve, 6 months behind. If nothing changes, Atlanta hits 60% retained by Q4 2026. The paying base is healthy but cannot sustain the marketplace alone as the free funnel dries up.

Zero-bid projects will keep growing as project mix shifts toward TBOs and renovations

Tenant Build-Outs grew 4.2pp in Atlanta in one year. These projects correlate with lowest sub engagement and highest GC churn. As more GCs post TBOs and less New Construction comes to market, the zero-bid rate will continue to rise — further suppressing GC retention.

NC's improving project mix may produce better retention outcomes than Atlanta

NC Education growing to 15% of projects, Restaurant/Retail declining. Education correlates with retained GCs and larger sub bids. If this trend holds, NC may show better GC retention than Atlanta over the next 2–3 quarters, despite being a smaller market with a thinner sub base.

The paying/free engagement gap will keep widening

Paying subs: 772–1,354 DL/sub, median bid growing. Basic/Free: 76 DL/sub and falling. At current rates the free tier becomes functionally inactive by mid-2026 in Atlanta. In NC it may already be there. The platform is becoming a paying-only marketplace by default.

Post-churn sub browsing pool will keep growing

63 subs browsing after cancellation in Jan 2025 grew to 109 by Apr 2026 — 73% growth in 16 months. These subs are maintaining 630–1,300 downloads/sub on free accounts. Without targeted re-engagement this pool will continue to grow quietly, representing unmonetized demand.

Out-of-market GCs will continue churning faster than locals

Atlanta churned GCs: Dallas (8), Chicago (6), Miami (6), Pittsburgh (5) are all churning at high rates. These national GCs post Atlanta projects but don't return — likely because they're not getting enough bids to justify the effort. As the free sub tier contracts further, out-of-market GC ROI declines.

Opportunities

Data-supported · actionable now
Opportunity A

109 post-churn subs are still actively browsing — warm re-engagement list

These cancelled subs download 630–1,300 files/month on free accounts. They demonstrated intent to pay (they subscribed), they know the platform, and they're still using it. This is the highest-conversion re-engagement cohort available. A targeted campaign with a clear value proposition (bid volume, project quality, recent wins in their trade) has a measurable conversion opportunity.

Opportunity B

Education and institutional projects drive better sub engagement and GC retention

Education projects in Atlanta attract 37% <$2M vs 48% for Retail. Municipal shows better band distribution. Retained GCs skew education/government. NC is already improving in this direction. Recruiting GCs who specialize in institutional work — or helping existing GCs access more institutional projects — would directly improve marketplace health metrics.

Opportunity C

The M-2 to M-1 window is the sub re-engagement trigger point

Downloads drop from M-2 to M-1 before cancellation — that's a 30–60 day window where the sub is still active but disengaging. A triggered intervention at this point (based on download activity decline + active subscription) could meaningfully reduce cancellations. The signal is detectable from existing platform data.

Opportunity D

Active 1yr+ paying subs are the highest-value cohort — and underrepresented

Active 1yr+ paying subs generate 81.5 bids/sub vs 33.3 for <1yr active and 24.0 for churned. Tenure compounds engagement. Programs that improve 6–12 month sub retention (onboarding quality, early bid success, trade-specific project matching) will produce outsized long-term bid volume.

Three things to do about it

Prioritized by data confidence and impact
01

Launch a post-churn re-engagement campaign for the 109 warm subs

These subs are downloading 630–1,300 files/month after cancellation — they haven't left the market, they've left the paywall. Pull the list from HubSpot (ChargeBee Status = Cancelled, last download activity within 90 days), segment by trade and revenue band, and run a targeted re-activation offer. Expected conversion rate is higher than cold outreach because intent is demonstrated. This is revenue recovery, not acquisition.

ATL: 109 subs · NC: growing pool · Est. 15–25% re-activation at historical paying rates
02

Build a download-decay trigger to catch at-risk subs 30–60 days before cancellation

Downloads drop before bids drop before cancellation. The M-1 signal is detectable from platform activity data. Build a trigger: if a paying sub's monthly downloads fall 50%+ month-over-month, flag for CSM outreach. This converts a reactive cancellation response into a proactive retention play. The intervention window is narrow — 30–60 days — but the data shows it exists and is consistent across both markets.

Signal: DL drop M-2 → M-1 · Window: 30–60 days pre-cancel · Applies to all paying subs
03

Target GC recruitment toward institutional and education projects to improve bid density

The data is clear: Education and Municipal projects attract better sub quality (larger rev bands), higher bid counts, and produce more retained GCs. Retail and Restaurant GCs are churning at higher rates and attracting primarily <$2M subs. Shift GC acquisition and activation efforts toward GCs who post institutional work — or build a project-type filter into the GC onboarding flow so the right GCs end up on the right projects. NC is already moving in this direction organically.

Education: 37% <$2M subs vs 48% for Retail · Retained GCs: 2–3× more institutional work
